Privacy policy

Last revised: 2026-10-17. Applies to the Apobri iPad/iPhone app and the Apobri Windows Host.

The short version

  • Apobri collects none of your data. There is no account, telemetry, analytics, crash upload or advertising, and no tracking. The developer never receives your screen, input, files or device information.
  • The screen, input, clipboard text and files go only to devices you paired, over your home network, Tailscale or another connection you set up yourself. Apobri has no accounts and no server that sees your screen. Only when your phone and PC cannot connect directly (for example you are away from home without Tailscale) does it use a Cloudflare connection service so the two devices can find each other; see How data travels.
  • The Windows Host itself does not open Internet connections. The exceptions are the Apobri service’s update check, which only asks GitHub whether a new Host version exists and sends nothing about you, and the Cloudflare connection service described above, which is used only when your devices cannot connect directly.
  • Everything is stored on your own devices, and you can revoke devices or delete the data at any time.

What we collect

Nothing. The iPad app’s Privacy Manifest declares: no tracking, no tracking domains, no collected data types.

How data travels

Your iPad connects directly to your Windows PC. Video, input and the data channels use WebRTC with DTLS encryption. On the same network or over Tailscale, no STUN or TURN server is used, so no third party learns your address.

When a direct connection is not possible (away from home, no Tailscale): the app uses a connection service run on Cloudflare (Cloudflare Workers) and public STUN servers (Cloudflare, Google) so the two devices can find each other and try to connect directly. If both networks are too restrictive, the picture may also be relayed through Cloudflare’s TURN service. These services can see a random identifier of your PC (not your name), the Internet addresses (IP) of your phone and PC, and when and how much data is exchanged. They cannot see your screen, input, files or clipboard: the connection data is end-to-end encrypted between your phone and PC (only the two paired devices hold the key), and the picture itself is DTLS-encrypted. Apobri does not keep these records and needs no account. Devices you have not paired never use this service.

One thing to state plainly: the current app does its signaling (the handshake that sets up the connection) over TLS (wss, the Host’s HTTPS port 8766, self-signed certificate) and pins the certificate fingerprint. Older app versions and the web client (PWA) still use the unencrypted HTTP port (default 8765) on your local network, which includes the challenge-response used when logging in with a pairing code (the code itself is not sent in the clear). On networks you do not trust, use the current app or Tailscale.

Toss and nearby direct links: what you put into Toss is synced between the devices you paired yourself (the PC, iPhone, iPad). Between a device and the PC it uses the same authenticated connection as above; when two Apple devices are near each other they connect directly over the local network or peer-to-peer Wi‑Fi (AWDL), encrypted with TLS (the keys come from a space key that only your devices hold). Apart from the relay service above (used only when a direct connection is not possible, and it cannot see the content either), nothing passes through any server. Toss keeps only what you explicitly put into it; ordinary copy and paste is not recorded. Note: after you remove a device it receives nothing new, but content already synced to that device cannot be taken back.

Pairing and direct connection: when you pair with a QR code, the code carries the fingerprint of this PC’s identity certificate, and the app checks it while pairing, so you know you are connected to the PC in front of you and not another machine on the same network. When the iPad or iPhone and the PC are on the same Wi‑Fi, they connect directly over the local network, not around through the Internet or another network path.

Background sync (iPhone / iPad): when the app is not on screen, iOS now and then lets Apobri run briefly in the background (“Background App Refresh”). Apobri uses that time only to catch up Toss with the PC you paired yourself: it fetches the list of changes and small items (up to 8 MB each), and hands larger unfinished transfers to the system’s background download. This happens only between your own devices and your PC; it does not contact other servers (except the relay service when a direct connection is not possible), does not play or record audio, and does not use your location. Without a paired PC there is no background work. You can turn it off for Apobri in iOS Settings > General > Background App Refresh.

Lock Screen and Dynamic Island (if your version of the app has this feature and it is on): while connected, the app shows a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island with the PC’s name, the connection quality and the progress of a Toss transfer. Anyone who can see your Lock Screen can see this while the phone is locked. It is updated only by the app on your phone (no push server) and ends when you leave the PC. You can turn it off in iOS Settings > Apobri > Live Activities.

No file size limit: files sent from the iPad to the PC are no longer limited in size; the only limit is the free space on the PC’s disk. If there is not enough space, the transfer stops, the unfinished temporary file is deleted, and you are told why.

The Host listens only on private addresses: this PC, your local network (10.x, 172.16–31.x, 192.168.x) and Tailscale (100.64.x–100.127.x). Over IPv6 it likewise serves only your home network and Tailscale: this PC, unique local addresses (fc00::/7, including Tailscale), link-local addresses, and addresses on the same home-network prefix as this PC; a direct connection from anywhere else on the Internet is refused. The pairing page and device management accept requests only from the PC itself.

The PC’s home-network addresses (including IPv6): the pairing page, the pairing QR code and the information the PC sends to the app after connecting carry this PC’s IPv4 address on your home network and, if it has one, a stable IPv6 address. Like the IPv4 address, they are used only so that your phone or iPad can find and reach this PC on the same network; they are stored on your own devices and sent nowhere else.

Automatic update check (Windows)

When the Apobri service (which keeps the Host up to date) is installed, it asks GitHub over HTTPS (api.github.com and GitHub’s download servers) for the newest Apobri Host release of one public repository every 6 hours, and when you press “Check for updates” / “Update” in the app. The request carries nothing about you or your PC besides what any web request carries (your IP address and a User-Agent: ApobriUpdater/1 header). A downloaded package is installed only if its signature verifies with the Apobri key built into the service. GitHub is an independent third-party service with its own privacy policy.

In-app purchases

The free trial and the unlock in the iPad/iPhone app are Apple in-app purchases. Apple handles payment and your Apple ID; we never receive your name, email or payment details. The app only asks the App Store whether this device’s Apple ID owns the trial or the unlock (and when the trial started).

What the iPad/iPhone app stores

  • Keychain: this device’s pairing key and pairing credentials.
  • App-local settings: the names and addresses of saved PCs, and connection and input preferences.
  • Files you receive are kept inside the app and in the Files app; they go to your Photos library only if you turn on “Save received photos & videos to Photos”.
  • Toss: the list of items in Toss, their thumbnails and the content you opened are cached in the app’s shared container (App Group). The Toss space key is kept in the Keychain for this device only; it is not synced to iCloud or included in backups.

This data does not leave your device, except what you yourself send to your PC during a connection. The Screen Broadcast extension sends the screen and sound of a broadcast you start yourself directly to the PC address you entered; it stores nothing and uses no server.

Screen content is never written to disk: frames go from the GPU to the encoder to the network. The Host also sets the Windows clipboard when you ask it to. Apobri sends none of this anywhere.

System sound (whatever the PC is playing, possibly private) is captured only during an authenticated WebRTC connection and only while the iPad's Sound switch is on, and travels over the same encrypted connection to that iPad; it is not recorded or written to disk, and in every other case the audio device is not opened at all. While the iPad confirms it is playing, the PC’s own speakers are muted; this is restored on disconnect, when sound is switched off, or after a restart (set AudioMutePcWhileConnected to false to turn this off). Turn it off in the iPad settings, or set "Audio": false in the Host config.

For the “Nearby computers” feature the Host announces itself on your local network with mDNS (_apobri._tcp): the computer name, private LAN IPv4 addresses, ports, the certificate fingerprint (public information), whether pairing is allowed, and a random host id. The announcement never leaves the local network and never contains the pairing code, keys, user name, file names or window titles. People on the same network can learn that this PC runs Apobri, but cannot connect: a new device still needs the pairing code or QR code shown on the PC. You can turn it off with "Discovery": false in config.json.

For Toss’s nearby direct links, the iPhone and iPad also announce the Bonjour service _apobri-toss._tcp on the local network and over peer-to-peer Wi‑Fi (AWDL). The announced name is a hash derived from the space key that changes every day; it contains no device name, user name or any fixed identifier. Without your space key nobody can tell whose device it is or connect to it.

Local helper programs the Host runs: ffmpeg.exe (video encoding), powershell (notifications), and, only if Tailscale is installed, tailscale status --json (read-only). None of them is told to contact the Internet.

Revoking and deleting

  • Revoke one device: the Apobri icon in the taskbar > “Paired devices”, or AppleBridge.Host.exe --revoke <id-prefix>. Revoke all: AppleBridge.Host.exe --revoke all. Connected sessions are dropped immediately. A revoked device gets no new Toss content (the PC hands a new space key to the devices left), but content already synced to it cannot be taken back.
  • Delete all Host data: answer yes to deleting settings, devices and logs when uninstalling, or delete %APPDATA%\AppleBridge yourself.
  • Delete app data: delete the Apobri app from your iPad; its Keychain items and local data are removed with it.

Third-party components

Apobri uses open-source components (for example SIPSorcery, QRCoder and Vortice in the Host, and a WebRTC library in the iPad app) and calls ffmpeg as a video encoder. They all run on your devices and do not send your data to their authors or any third party. See Licenses and third-party notices for the list and licenses. Tailscale, if you choose to use it, is a separate third-party service with its own privacy policy.

Children’s privacy

Apobri is not directed at children and collects no personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If the policy changes materially, we will update this page and the revision date above.
